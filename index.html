Services

Every engagement you need is here.

Clients usually come for one of the four engagements below. If your question sits outside them, we will say so.

For employers

Talent acquisition

A good hire is decided before the job is posted, and it has to be lawful in the country where the person will work. We help you describe the role honestly, decide what is essential, and run a process that candidates in any market experience as respectful.

Companies that hire across borders often ask us to run the search or to tighten the one they have: clearer questions, a fair comparison between candidates, and a clean handoff to the hiring manager. If you are the person looking, career coaching is the engagement built for you.

For employers

Payroll management

People remember a payroll mistake longer than they remember a raise. Pay has to be accurate, on time, and explainable in the currency and the rules of the country where it is earned.

Your accountant and local payroll provider stay in place. Our role is the HR side of pay across countries: classifications, deductions, bonus runs, and the questions managers get from their teams. We would rather find a problem before an employee does.

For employers

Organizational development

Growth across countries is usually what exposes the cracks a company has been stepping over. Decision rights get muddy. Managers in different time zones invent their own version of the job. Meetings multiply, and the real work waits.

We spend time in that real work, then recommend a short list of changes. The aim is a company that functions on an ordinary working day, with a management group that knows who decides what.

For professionals

Career coaching

Clients come before an international search, in the middle of a relocation, or when a job they already hold has turned confusing. Some pay for the coaching themselves. Others arrive because their employer has asked us to support a manager who now leads people in more than one country.

The work is structured and direct. Together we name a few aims and continue until those aims are met, or until you decide to set them down. Expect a working conversation with someone who has sat on both sides of the hiring table.

Approach

What working together looks like.

Three steps, the same ones every time. A week of work and a full quarter both follow this path.

  1. 01

    A first conversation

    Tell us what is happening, and which time zone you keep. The call is free, and it is also a chance for both sides to decide if the work belongs here.

  2. 02

    A scope in writing

    If we proceed, you receive a short letter: the task, the fee, the timing, and the name of the advisor responsible.

  3. 03

    Work you can use

    You get advice in plain language, plus the documents or decisions underneath it. Then we stop, unless there is a reason to continue.

About the firm

A small firm, on purpose.

The founders spent years inside corporate HR departments, often called in after a hire, a pay decision, or a reorganization had already gone wrong. Hiringresource opened in 2009 so the advice could arrive earlier, in language a busy owner or an individual professional could actually use. The work soon crossed borders, because the clients did.

The practice is still small for that reason. A named advisor stays with each engagement, whether the client is in one city or spread across several. Fees, timing, and scope are written down before the work begins. If the fit is wrong, we say so before that letter is drafted.

A recommendation is only finished when someone can act on it in their own workplace.

Employers

Owners, founders, and HR leads at companies with people in more than one country, often between 15 and 400 people, who want senior counsel without building a global HR department.
